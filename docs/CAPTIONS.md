Comparison: Post-clearing responses of the equal-weight remote group at buses 2, 3, and 7 for uniform active-power filter time constants of 1.16, 1.18, and 1.20 s. A 20-pu passive shunt at bus 4 is removed after 0.15 s while line 4–5 is opened. The reactive-power filter time constant is 0.02 s for all four converters. The panels show the mean internal-angle change in turns and the mean internal-frequency deviation relative to 60 Hz. A locally stable post-fault equilibrium and a saddle with one unstable mode are verified for each case; their projected remote mean-angle separation is 0.139445 rad. Observation horizons differ and are stated in the legend. Negative rotation is observed over the finite simulated intervals; indefinite persistence is not established.

Individual-machine responses: Internal-angle change, internal-frequency deviation, terminal-voltage magnitude, and capacitor-side active power of each converter after clearing. Horizontal dotted lines in the power panel indicate active-power references. Internal angles and frequencies are distinguished from terminal-voltage phase and its derivative. The curves use stored detailed-model samples without additional plotting decimation.

Conditional basin comparison with fault-on overlay: Conditional finite-time resynchronization regions of the post-fault system, with the physical event trajectory projected onto remote mean internal angle and frequency deviation. Purple curves show the 0.15-s fault-on interval, triangles mark fault application, white circles mark clearing, and black curves show the post-fault response. Fault-on samples were recalculated using the matching pre-fault equilibrium and checked against the reference full-state endpoints and the start of the saved post-fault trajectory. The background fixes the initial values of the other 46 differential states at the post-fault equilibrium; the event trajectory can leave this initial-state section. The fault-on system has different equations from the post-fault system, so crossing a background transition during the fault does not determine the subsequent outcome.

Fault-on comparison: Enlarged phase-plane trajectories and frequency time histories for the three active-power filter time constants during the fault. Time is relative to clearing, with fault application at −0.15 s. Triangles and circles indicate application and clearing, respectively. The angle origin is the post-fault stable equilibrium. These curves resolve the small differences in the clearing coordinates; they do not establish inclusion relations between full-state attraction basins.
